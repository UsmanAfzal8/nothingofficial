Design, materials and portability

Over-ear headphones make a stronger visual statement than earbuds. Nothing Headphone (a) is built for people who want the brand’s clean, transparent-inspired identity without carrying the largest possible premium audio product. Its cups and headband should be considered together: the best design is the one you can wear, fold or store without turning daily transport into a chore.

Headphone 1 takes the more expressive flagship route. A premium-looking headphone can be satisfying to own because it feels like a deliberate object rather than a disposable accessory. That does not mean it is automatically more practical. Measure the space in your backpack, consider whether you need a case, and check whether the headband remains comfortable when worn with glasses or a cap.

Both models deserve basic care in Pakistan’s heat and dust. Do not leave them on a car dashboard, store them while damp after exercise, or place heavy objects on the ear cups. Wipe the pads with a soft, dry cloth and keep charging ports free of debris. Cushions are consumable parts, so good care extends the period before replacement is needed.

The larger over-ear format gives both headphones room to create a more spacious presentation than small earbuds. You may notice deeper bass, more relaxed vocals and a wider sense of left-to-right placement, especially with a good seal. The result still depends on the recording, streaming quality, fit and the equaliser profile in the Nothing X app.

Headphone 1 is aimed at listeners who want a more premium sound conversation. It may give you more adjustment headroom, stronger separation or a more considered acoustic presentation, depending on its selected mode and the source. That is valuable for albums, instrumental music and long listening, but do not assume every track will sound better automatically. Use familiar recordings and compare at equal volume.

Streaming quality, equaliser settings and listening habits

The headphones cannot restore detail that was removed by a low-quality source. If you compare the models using a compressed social-media clip, inconsistent volume can be more noticeable than the actual headphone difference. Use a familiar album, a clear podcast and a quiet video. Start with both products at the same approximate loudness and make notes after several minutes rather than choosing the louder pair.

The Nothing X equaliser is useful because personal taste changes by genre. A small bass adjustment may help electronic music, while a modest reduction can make spoken voices clearer. Avoid extreme settings that create distortion or make the driver work harder than necessary. If you change phones, repeat the check because equaliser profiles may be stored differently or may not apply in every app.

Listening habits affect value more than a premium label. A person who wears headphones for a whole workday needs stable comfort, low heat and controls that do not interrupt focus. A person who listens for twenty minutes on a commute may care more about quick pairing, compact storage and price. Write down your normal use before paying for features you may never activate.

Passive isolation, glasses and outside awareness

Over-ear headphones reduce some sound even when ANC is disabled. This passive isolation can be useful in an office or on a bus, and it consumes no battery. It can also reduce awareness, so keep the volume moderate near traffic, bikes, crossings and other situations where an outside warning matters. Transparency mode is not a substitute for looking around.

Glasses can create a small gap between the ear cushion and the side of your head. That gap may reduce bass and ANC, particularly when the frame arms are thick. Test each headphone with the glasses you normally wear. If you use a hijab, cap or other head covering, check that the band and cups sit comfortably without forcing the seal or creating excessive pressure.

Hair, earrings and long listening sessions also affect fit. A cup that seals perfectly for one person may leak for another. Do not interpret a weak bass response as a sound defect until you have tried the headband adjustment and both ear cups. Fit is a performance setting, not merely a comfort preference.

Maintenance and long-term ownership

Battery-powered headphones age gradually, so long-term care matters. Avoid leaving them at zero percent for weeks, and do not keep them permanently plugged in in a hot room. Store them somewhere dry and shaded. If you will not use them for a while, leave a moderate charge and wake them occasionally rather than abandoning a completely empty battery.

Clean the ear pads with the manufacturer’s recommended method and never pour liquid into a cup, control or charging opening. Sweat and skin oils can change pad texture over time. If a pad becomes loose, cracked or permanently flattened, ask the authorised service channel whether replacement parts are available rather than fitting an incompatible pad that changes the seal.

Firmware updates can improve stability or add features, but they cannot repair physical damage. Update through a stable phone connection with sufficient battery, and avoid beginning an update immediately before a trip. Keep the app installed long enough to save or note your preferred settings.

How to compare both models in one listening session

If you can try both, use a simple routine. First check that both headphones are charged and paired to the same phone. Next listen to a spoken recording, a familiar vocal track and an instrumental or bass-heavy track. Switch ANC off, then on, then transparency, allowing a moment for the mode to settle. Finally test a short call and the physical controls.

Write down four scores: comfort after thirty minutes, voice clarity, isolation in your normal environment and ease of control. Add the current price and warranty as separate scores. This prevents an impressive first song from hiding a headband problem or a difficult return process. If you cannot test before ordering, choose the seller with the clearest written return policy and preserve the packaging.

Do not compare only the headline maximum. A theoretical ANC number, a maximum battery number and a codec label are measured under conditions. A headphone with a good seal and comfortable moderate volume may be the better daily performer than a more expensive model that is left at home because it feels heavy.

ANC and transparency for Pakistan travel

Active noise cancellation is useful when you face consistent low-frequency noise: a bus engine, an air conditioner, a fan, an aeroplane cabin or a train. Headphone (a) covers the everyday ANC use case. Headphone 1 is the higher-tier model and is intended to provide the more ambitious noise-control experience, but the real advantage depends on the ear-cup seal and your environment.

ANC is not a force field. It will not reliably remove every voice, horn, sudden door slam or motorcycle. Over-ear cups already block some sound passively, and a loose fit can undermine both passive isolation and electronic cancellation. Adjust the headband, check that the cups surround your ears fully and use a consistent song or noise source when comparing modes.

Transparency mode matters just as much as ANC for city life. You may need to hear a colleague, a delivery call, a traffic signal or someone speaking at a counter. A good transparency mode lets you stay aware without taking the headphones off. Test it with speech and environmental sound rather than judging it only from an app label.

Bluetooth codecs and phone compatibility

Codec support should be assessed with your phone rather than in isolation. Android devices may expose more Bluetooth codec choices than iPhone, and the operating system can change which option is actually active. A higher-data codec can be useful with compatible hardware and clean radio conditions, but it can also use more power or become less stable in a crowded wireless environment.

iPhone owners can pair compatible Bluetooth headphones, but they should not purchase based on an Android-only codec expectation. The important questions are whether the headphones connect reliably, whether the app provides the settings you need, whether calls work, and whether the default tuning suits your music.

Nothing Phone users may receive the smoothest brand experience through Nothing X and device-level integrations. Both headphones should still be considered normal Bluetooth products for Samsung, Pixel, Xiaomi and other Android devices. Keep firmware current, grant the app the required Bluetooth permissions and test multipoint with the actual laptop and phone you use.

ANC, transparency, calls, high volume and custom processing reduce runtime. Calls, microphones and online meetings

Keep the microphone openings clean and avoid covering them with a scarf or hand. If the app offers call-noise settings, test them in the environment where you actually work. Do not wait until an important meeting to discover that a laptop selected its internal microphone instead of the headphone.

Comfort, clamp and ear-cup fit

Comfort is the most important difference you cannot settle from a product page. Headphone (a) may appeal to listeners who want a lighter, simpler over-ear experience. Headphone 1 may feel more substantial and immersive, but greater material presence can also mean more weight or clamp for some head shapes.

Try each model for at least thirty minutes. Notice pressure on the top of your head, heat around the ears, glasses-arm pressure and whether the cups stay sealed when you look down. A perfect first impression is not enough if the headphones become uncomfortable after an hour. On the other hand, a firm initial seal may improve ANC and bass once the headband is adjusted.

Do not stretch the headband aggressively or store it under pressure. Folding hinges and ear-cup swivels are designed for normal use, not repeated force. If the pads become sweaty, let them dry before closing the case or pouch. Comfort is partly a maintenance issue because dirty or compressed pads change the fit over time.

Gaming and latency expectations
