How the periscope changes composition

A long lens is not only for distant subjects. It changes the relationship between foreground and background. At 3x, a row of lights, a building façade or a person against a busy street can appear more layered and orderly. Move your feet, try different distances and use the lens to decide what belongs in the frame.

Keep the camera level for architecture and use grid lines if they help. For a portrait, leave room above the head and avoid placing the subject too close to the frame edge. For a product, keep the phone parallel to the surface so text and shape do not distort.

Practice before an important event. Learn how the phone switches between cameras, where the exposure control sits and how long it takes to save a high-resolution file. Familiarity is more valuable than a higher zoom number when a moment lasts one second.

Focus, motion and stabilisation

OIS reduces small hand movements, but it cannot freeze a moving subject. Use a brighter location, faster shutter behaviour and several frames for people, birds or vehicles. Tap the subject and watch the focus indicator. If a fence or branch is between you and the subject, tap again to prevent the camera from locking onto the foreground.

At 6x, a tiny movement becomes a large framing shift. Brace your elbows, lean against a wall or use a stable surface. A timer can help with a building or landscape. For video, avoid fast zooms and use a stable cut between clips instead.

Atmospheric haze matters too. Dust, humidity and heat shimmer lower contrast before the light reaches the lens. If a distant scene looks soft in every frame, the air may be the limiting factor rather than the sensor.

Colour, HDR and exposure

Compare the main, telephoto and ultra-wide cameras on a scene with bright sky, shade and coloured objects. Look for a consistent white balance, natural skin and controlled highlights. A vivid preview can hide clipped clouds or unnatural foliage, so inspect the saved file at normal size.

Tap the bright area when a white wall or sky is losing texture, then make a small exposure adjustment. In shade, protect faces from becoming too dark. HDR can recover some range, but it may create halos around branches or buildings if the scene has movement.

Photograph a printed page, a face and a textured object. These simple tests reveal focus, fine detail, sharpening and colour better than a dramatic sunset. Keep original files before applying filters or heavy crops.

Selfies, group photos and ultra-wide use

The ultra-wide camera is useful for groups in a small room, interiors and buildings that cannot fit at 1x. Keep faces away from the extreme edges because wide-angle stretching can be unflattering. Step back where possible and use a moderate lens for people.

For group photos, use a stable surface or ask another person to hold the phone, check that everyone is focused and take more than one frame. Bright windows behind the group can confuse exposure, so turn people toward softer light.

Selfie quality depends on focus, light and processing as much as resolution. Test indoor and outdoor faces, backlighting and video calls. A phone with a higher rear-camera specification may not automatically be the best video-call tool.

Battery, heat and storage during camera use

High-resolution photos, 4K video and repeated zoom processing consume storage and can warm the phone. Avoid leaving it on a car dashboard in direct sun. If it becomes hot, pause, remove a thick case and let it cool naturally. Do not cool a hot phone with water or a freezer.

Before a wedding, trip or event, charge the phone, clear safe storage and confirm that backup is working. Video files can fill a drive quickly. Keep at least one copy of important work until you have reviewed focus, audio and exposure.

Camera hardware explained

The 50MP main camera is the everyday workhorse. It is the lens to choose for mixed indoor light, food, family snapshots and scenes where you need the most forgiving exposure. OIS helps with hand movement, but it does not freeze a moving person or remove blur from a shaky subject.

The 50MP periscope uses a folded optical path to provide a longer focal length in a phone body. Nothing publishes a 75mm focal length and 3x optical zoom, which gives portraits and distant details a more natural perspective. OIS helps keep the preview and capture steadier, particularly when the subject is static.

The 8MP ultra-wide is useful for rooms, buildings, groups and landscapes when you cannot step back. Its smaller sensor and lower resolution can be more visible in low light. A complete camera review should compare all three lenses rather than treating the longest zoom as the only result.

What 3x optical zoom feels like

At 3x, you can frame a face from a comfortable distance without the stretched perspective of a close wide-angle shot. You can isolate a sign across a street, photograph a stage performer without standing directly at the front, or compress layers of an architectural scene.

Tap the subject and wait for focus. Support the phone with both hands, especially in shade. Take several frames if the subject is important. A single press can move the phone enough to soften fine detail, even with optical stabilisation.

Compare 1x and 3x in the same light. The telephoto can look more natural, but the main camera may gather more light at dusk. The best mode depends on the scene, not a rule that the dedicated camera must always win.

6x and 60x: realistic expectations

6x in-sensor zoom can extend the periscope’s reach in bright daylight. Use it for distant signs, details on a building or a subject across a field. Hold the phone steady and shoot multiple frames. Haze, dust and heat shimmer become more obvious as the distance grows.

60x ultra zoom is heavily processed. It can help you frame an object, read a large high-contrast sign or record information you cannot approach, but fine textures may smear and edges may show halos. Do not treat the highest number as equivalent to a professional long lens.

Review the saved file at normal size. A zoomed preview can look impressive while the final image is too soft for printing. The upper range is most useful when it lets you see or document something otherwise out of reach.

Portraits and people

The periscope is especially useful for portraits. Stand far enough away to keep the person comfortable, tap the eye and check the background. Use 3x when light is good, and switch to the main camera when the room is dark or the subject moves quickly.

Portrait blur can struggle around hair, glasses, patterned clothing and hands. Leave a little space around the subject and inspect the edges before sharing. Open shade is often better than harsh noon sunlight because it reduces facial contrast.

For children, pets and events, shutter speed matters more than zoom. Take a burst or several frames. A sharp 1x image is more valuable than a blurred 3x portrait.

Daylight, architecture and travel

Daylight gives the camera its widest useful range. Start with the ultra-wide for the entire building or landscape, then use 3x for a detail. Compare colour, sky texture and shadow detail between cameras because the lenses may process the same scene differently.

In a city, signs, balconies and distant façades make good test subjects. Keep the phone level to avoid leaning vertical lines, and tap a bright area if the sky is washing out. A small exposure adjustment can preserve clouds and white walls.

For travel, save both a wide context photograph and a telephoto detail. This produces a more meaningful story than many similar crops. Keep the original files and back them up before deleting shots to make space.

Low-light camera performance

Night zoom is difficult because the periscope receives less light and the shutter cannot stay open when people move. OIS and computational processing help, but a stable, well-lit subject is still required. Start at 3x under street lighting and compare the main camera crop.

Use night mode for static scenes and hold the phone against a solid surface. Avoid touching it immediately after pressing the shutter. Check signs and bright lamps for halos, and look at shadow texture rather than judging only how bright the image appears.

At night, the main camera is often the safer option. The periscope is a specialist tool, not a guarantee of clean long-range photographs in darkness.

Video and 4K recording

Nothing’s official product information describes 4K recording with AI-powered stability enhancements. Open the camera app on the exact unit to confirm resolution, frame rates and which lenses are available for video. Video tests should include focus, exposure, stabilisation and audio, not just resolution.

Record a walking clip, a static speech clip and a short zoom transition. Watch for exposure jumps, focus hunting and a distracting change in colour when switching lenses. A stable edit between two clips often looks better than continuous digital zoom.

In Pakistan’s heat, long video sessions can warm the phone. Keep it ventilated, use sufficient storage and avoid recording in direct sunlight. Test a microphone or Bluetooth accessory before an important event.

Display, performance and battery

The phone combines a large high-refresh-rate AMOLED display with a Snapdragon 7s Gen 3 5G platform. It should suit daily apps, streaming, moderate gaming and camera processing. Test outdoor readability, touch response, auto-brightness and heat during a sustained game.

Battery life depends on signal, brightness, refresh rate, GPS, camera and background sync. Measure a normal day on your SIM rather than quoting a universal result. A weak indoor signal can drain the phone faster than a strong Wi-Fi day.

Confirm charger and accessory contents. Use a compatible adapter, keep the port dry and avoid charging in a hot car. Add the cost of a case, protector and power bank to your budget if your routine requires them.

Software, Glyph and Essential Space

Nothing OS provides the interface, camera software, Glyph controls and supported Essential workflows. Features can vary with model, region and update. Check the installed version and test notifications, Bluetooth, banking, Android Auto where applicable and the camera after setup.

If you use Essential Space or the Essential Key, review privacy and sync behaviour before saving sensitive documents or recordings. AI summaries should be checked for names, dates, amounts and addresses, especially with mixed English, Urdu or Roman Urdu.

Camera privacy and responsible zoom

A periscope camera makes it easier to photograph people, homes and documents from a distance. Use that capability responsibly. Ask before publishing an identifiable person, and be especially careful around schools, clinics, workplaces and private events. A camera setting is not permission.

Check the background before sharing a frame. Addresses, vehicle plates, tickets and screens can reveal more than intended. Crop or blur sensitive details, and keep the original only when there is a legitimate reason. Do not photograph passwords, one-time codes or identity documents into a general gallery.

Who should buy the Pro?

The phone suits a buyer who travels, photographs portraits, attends events, shoots architecture or wants more framing choices without a dedicated camera. It also suits someone who enjoys the Nothing interface and can obtain a clear local warranty. It is less compelling for a buyer who never uses zoom, needs guaranteed local repair or is stretching the budget to cover an unclear import cost.

Prioritise reliability, total cost, support and battery before decorative features. The periscope is a real advantage, but it should solve a recurring problem in your life.

Return-window checklist

PTA, warranty and imported stock

PTA status belongs to the exact device and IMEI process. Ask whether both IMEIs are handled where required, who pays registration and what happens if the process is rejected. Keep your invoice private and do not publish the IMEI.

Clarify whether the warranty is official, distributor-backed or shop-only. Ask about service location, turnaround, liquid damage, screen damage and return shipping. A lower imported price can disappear after an overseas repair.

Test calls, mobile data, hotspot, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, camera, charging, speakers and microphone during the return window. Check the model code against the box and invoice before accepting the unit as new.

Storage and buying configuration

Choose storage for photos, 4K video, games, offline maps and WhatsApp media. Telephoto photography often creates several trial frames, so a higher capacity may be worthwhile for a long ownership period. Confirm RAM and storage because regional configurations differ.

Keep free storage for system updates and camera processing. Back up important images to a trusted location, but do not delete originals until they have uploaded successfully. A full phone can make every camera and update task more frustrating.
