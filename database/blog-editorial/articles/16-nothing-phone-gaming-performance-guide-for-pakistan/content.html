How to read official 120 fps claims

Nothing’s current Phone 4a page reports a 116.7 fps average during a 30-minute BGMI test in the game’s 120 fps high-frame-rate mode, connected to Wi-Fi under default settings. That is useful evidence for one controlled configuration. It is not a promise that every title, graphics preset, map, software update or mobile-network session will hold the same result.

Phone 3a’s official page reports an 88.7 fps average during one hour of BGMI in its 90 fps mode under the stated test conditions. The longer test and different target mean the two published figures should not be combined into a simple percentage comparison.

Phone 4b is advertised for supported 120 fps gameplay with a 1,000 Hz touch-sampling claim and 4,400 mm² cooling system. Its public product wording does not mean all games expose 120 fps. Always check the title’s own settings and supported-device policy.

Frame rate, refresh rate and frame pacing

Frames per second measure how many images the game renders. Refresh rate measures how often the panel can show a new image. A 120 Hz screen creates room for 120 fps, but it cannot manufacture missing game frames. A title capped at 60 fps remains a 60 fps title on a 120 Hz panel.

Frame pacing describes how evenly frames arrive. A stable 60 fps can feel better than a counter jumping between 75 and 120 because irregular delivery produces visible judder and inconsistent input response. Average FPS hides slow frames, loading stalls and thermal drops.

When comparing phones, observe the full frame-time pattern during combat, driving, large scenes and effects—not only a lobby counter. The Nothing Phone display and refresh-rate guide explains the panel side in more detail.

Chipset names do not tell the entire story

The processor defines much of the ceiling, but the phone maker controls power limits, cooling, memory, storage and software scheduling. The same chipset in two devices can sustain different speeds. Game optimisation can also favour one graphics driver or model profile.

CPU demand often appears in simulation, crowds, physics and high frame-rate targets. GPU demand rises with resolution, shadows, effects and texture filtering. Storage affects installation, loading and asset streaming, while memory pressure can force apps to reload.

Do not compare “up to” clock speeds as if all cores run at that speed continuously. Mobile processors boost briefly and adjust according to workload, temperature and power.

Sustained performance and thermal throttling

A phone can start a benchmark quickly and slow after heat accumulates. Thermal throttling is a protective reduction in CPU, GPU or charging power. It is expected safety behavior, though excessive drops can hurt play.

Test for at least 20–30 minutes and preferably the duration of a normal match sequence. Watch for declining frame rate, dimming, touch inconsistency, network changes and battery drain. A one-minute score proves peak behavior, not sustained performance.

Surface warmth alone does not reveal internal temperature. Some effective cooling systems spread heat across the body, making more area feel warm while keeping the chip controlled. Stop if the phone displays a temperature warning, becomes painful to handle, swells, smells unusual or behaves unsafely.

Pakistan heat changes the gaming result

Manufacturer tests are normally performed in controlled environments. A non-air-conditioned room in Karachi, Lahore, Multan or another hot location can begin with less thermal headroom. Direct sunlight adds panel and body heat before a match starts.

Play indoors away from sun, remove a thick insulating case when safe, and place the phone on a dry stable surface between matches. Do not put the phone in a refrigerator, freezer, wet cloth or against ice. Rapid cooling can create condensation and damage.

A reputable clip-on cooler may help sustained performance, but pressure, condensation and blocked controls are risks. Use moderate cooling and stop if moisture appears.

Charging while gaming

Gaming produces processor and display heat; charging produces battery and conversion heat. Combining them can raise temperature and trigger reduced performance or charging speed. Fast charging is especially unnecessary when the phone already has enough charge for the session.

Begin with a comfortable battery level, use a known compatible charger and cable, and avoid covering the device. If charging is necessary, a lower workload or lower frame-rate cap may provide a more stable experience.

Do not route a damaged cable across your hands or use an unverified charger. Battery swelling, unusual smell, port heat or repeated charging interruptions require stopping and professional inspection. See the Nothing Phone charging guide for battery-health practices.

Graphics settings that improve stability

Start from the game’s recommended preset, then select the frame-rate target before raising visual options. Resolution scale, shadows, reflections, anti-aliasing, crowd density and effects can have different costs. Change one setting at a time so the effect is visible.

For competitive shooters, prioritise stable frame rate and touch response over maximum shadows. For story games, a stable 30 or 60 fps with richer visuals may be preferable. There is no universal “best” preset.

Download resource packs before testing. Shader compilation and background asset downloads can cause temporary stutter that is not steady-state performance.

Game support and device whitelists

Some games expose 90 or 120 fps only to approved device profiles. The phone may have sufficient hardware while the option remains unavailable until the developer validates it. Editing configuration files or impersonating another device can violate terms, create crashes or risk an account.

Check the official game release notes and in-game settings after updates. A server-side change can enable or disable a mode without a Nothing OS update.

Do not sideload modified APKs promising unlocked FPS, free currency or lower ping. They can steal accounts, install malware or trigger bans. Use Google Play and the publisher’s official distribution channel.

Touch sampling, latency and controls

Touch sampling measures how frequently the digitizer checks for input. It is separate from display refresh and total game latency. Nothing’s instant high-rate claims may apply during supported gaming conditions rather than continuously.

Total response includes the touchscreen, operating system, game engine, CPU/GPU rendering, display refresh and network for server-confirmed actions. A high touch number cannot compensate for unstable frame pacing or 150 ms network delay.

Test swipes near every edge, multi-touch, aim movement and on-screen buttons. A poor screen protector or replacement display can reduce touch quality even when the phone model originally had strong hardware.

Ping is not FPS

FPS is local visual performance. Ping is round-trip network delay to the game server. A phone can render a smooth 120 fps while characters teleport because the connection is unstable. It can also show low ping while the GPU struggles.

Pakistan latency depends on server region, internet routing, mobile signal, Wi-Fi interference and congestion. A nearby-looking server label does not guarantee the shortest route. Packet loss and jitter can be more disruptive than average ping.

Test Wi-Fi and mobile data at the same location and time. Use 5 GHz Wi-Fi near the router when coverage is strong, pause background downloads and avoid a crowded channel. Do not buy a phone based on an unsupported claim that one operator always gives better game ping.

Mobile data, Wi-Fi and background traffic

A strong signal indicator does not prove low latency. Network load, tower handover, router bufferbloat and international routing can all matter. Voice calls, cloud backup and another device downloading can create spikes.

For a fair comparison, use the same server, account, match mode and network. Record average ping, jitter and packet loss over several sessions. One unusually good match is not a baseline.

Keep the phone’s system time automatic, update carrier settings where applicable and restart the router before diagnosing. VPNs usually add routing overhead unless they happen to improve a poor route; they are not guaranteed gaming accelerators.

RAM, RAM Booster and multitasking

Physical RAM holds active game data. Enough RAM helps prevent background apps from being killed, but extra capacity does not directly increase GPU speed. A game limited by graphics performance will not gain frames simply because the model has more memory.

RAM Booster uses storage as extended memory. It can help retain applications in some cases but is slower than physical RAM and creates additional storage activity. Treat “up to” combined RAM marketing separately from installed physical RAM.

Close screen recorders, overlays and heavy background apps during controlled testing. Normal users do not need to force-stop every service; aggressive cleaner apps can create more reloads and battery use.

128 GB versus 256 GB for gaming

Modern games can require a large base installation plus texture packs, maps, language files and temporary update space. Screen recordings and social clips add quickly. A nearly full phone can struggle to download or unpack updates even when the listed package appears smaller.

128 GB can work for one or two major titles with disciplined photo and download management. 256 GB is safer for several large games, offline media and recording. Remember that usable capacity is lower than the marketed number after formatting and system files.

Storage speed also affects loading and installation. Phone 3’s UFS 4.0 and Phone 4a’s UFS 3.1 are relevant platform differences, but game code and asset design determine the visible gain. The next editorial guide covers storage choices in detail.

Battery drain and session planning

High brightness, high frame rate, strong GPU load, mobile data and speakers all consume power. A 120 fps competitive session can drain much faster than a 30 fps strategy game. Battery capacity alone does not prove gaming endurance.

Measure percentage used over a repeatable 30- or 60-minute session at the same brightness, network and settings. Record temperature and starting charge. Avoid comparing a new phone completing setup with an older settled device.

Battery saver may cap performance or refresh rate, so note whether it is active. For travel or load shedding, a stable 60 fps preset can extend play more usefully than chasing a short 120 fps burst.

Audio, haptics and communication

Stereo separation helps locate events, but speaker quality changes with grip and orientation. Test whether hands block a speaker. Wired USB-C audio or low-latency wireless accessories can offer better privacy, though Bluetooth codec latency varies.

Phone 4a advertises an X-axis linear motor for responsive haptics. Game support and vibration settings determine whether it is used meaningfully. Strong vibration can also consume some power and distract competitive users.

For team chat, test microphone clarity, echo and background noise. A headset should not obstruct charging or make the phone uncomfortable during long sessions.

Nothing OS gaming settings

Software labels can change between Nothing OS releases, so search Settings for game mode, refresh rate, battery and notifications. Enable only features that solve a problem. Performance modes may increase heat and drain; notification blocking can hide an urgent call.

Keep Nothing OS and the game updated through official channels, but read release notes before a tournament or ranked session. A fresh update can bring optimisation while also requiring shader rebuilding or introducing a title-specific issue.

Restart before diagnosing persistent stutter, verify free storage and let app optimisation finish after an OTA. The Nothing Phone software-updates guide explains safe update and rollback boundaries.

How to benchmark honestly

  1. Record exact phone model, storage/RAM variant and Nothing OS build.
  2. Record game version, server, graphics preset and frame-rate option.
  3. Use the same brightness, audio, network and room conditions.
  4. Begin from a similar battery percentage and device temperature.
  5. Test real gameplay for at least 20–30 minutes.
  6. Record average performance plus frame drops, heat and battery use.
  7. Repeat on more than one day before drawing a conclusion.
  8. Disclose external coolers, performance modes and overlays.

Synthetic benchmarks are useful for isolating CPU, GPU or sustained load, but they do not reproduce every game engine. Do not publish a score without the software version and conditions. Never compare a cooled test with an uncooled phone without saying so.

How to test a used Nothing Phone for gaming

Inspect the display for replacement history, burn-in, tint and touch gaps. Confirm the exact model and storage. Check battery condition, charging port, speakers, buttons, Wi-Fi, mobile data and frame stability during the buyer’s actual game.

Run at least 20 minutes from a normal room temperature. Watch for sudden dimming, shutdown, ghost touches, network drops and unusually rapid battery decline. A reset phone may still be downloading assets, so prepare the test with the seller.

Check PTA status independently and obtain the invoice and repair disclosure. A low price does not compensate for a weak battery or non-original panel when gaming is the purchase reason. Use the Nothing Phone authenticity checklist for the wider inspection.
